# Krug's Usability Tests

## Don't Make Me Think

Every question that appears in the user's head is friction. Labels should be
self-evident. If a label needs an explanation, simplify the label.

- Prefer “Sign in” over “Access your account portal”.
- Prefer “Save” over “Persist”.
- Error text says what to do, not only what failed.

## Users Scan, They Don't Read

- Put the most important fact first.
- Use headings, short rows and whitespace.
- Do not hide the primary action below a wall of explanation.
- Do not write marketing copy inside a utility screen.

## Users Satisfice

They pick the first plausible option. The primary path must be the obvious one,
and the alternatives must not look equally primary.

## Get Rid of Half the Words

Remove welcome copy, filler, repeated labels and instructions nobody reads.
Then remove half of what remains. Brevity must not remove safety or legal
information.

## The Trunk Test

Drop a person on any screen. They should answer:

1. What product/screen is this?
2. What page am I on?
3. What are the major sections?
4. What can I do here?
5. Where am I in the hierarchy?
6. Where is search/help if the product has it?

If the title, back path and primary action do not answer these, the page fails.

## Small-Screen and Touch

- One clear title per screen.
- One primary task; secondary actions are quieter.
- A predictable way back.
- A tap target large enough for the target's physical size.
- Do not put two competing navigation models around a short flow.
- Keep destructive actions away from the primary action's resting thumb path.

## Copy Patterns

| Bad | Better |
| --- | --- |
| “Validation error” | “Enter a password with at least 8 characters.” |
| “No data” | “No recordings yet. Tap Record to start.” |
| “Are you sure?” | “Delete this recording? This cannot be undone.” |
| “Welcome to Settings!” | “Settings” |
| “Submit” | “Save changes” |
